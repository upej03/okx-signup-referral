## OKX KYC: What You Need And How Long It Takes

KYC (Know Your Customer) verification is mandatory on OKX. You can browse markets and prices without it, but you cannot deposit, trade, or withdraw until you verify.

### Documents you'll need

The exact documents vary by region, but generally:

- **Government-issued photo ID**: passport, national ID card, or driver's license. Must be unexpired and clearly readable.
- **Proof of address**: a recent utility bill or bank statement showing your name and address, typically dated within the last 3 months.
- **Selfie / live facial scan**: taken in real time through the app or webcam.
- **SSN for U.S. residents**: U.S. users must enter their Social Security Number as part of regulatory compliance.

EU and Asian users usually need just a photo ID plus address verification. U.S. users need SSN plus a driver's license or state ID. Some regions have additional requirements for higher tiers.

### How long verification takes

OKX processes most KYC applications in 10 to 30 minutes. Complex cases — name mismatches, document quality issues, or additional compliance checks — can take up to 24 hours. You'll see the status update in your account dashboard.

### Common rejection reasons

Most rejections come down to:

- Blurry or glare-affected ID photos
- Documents that don't match your registration info (name spelling, address, country)
- Expired IDs
- Missing proof of address

If you get rejected, retake the photos in good natural light, make sure everything matches, and resubmit. Persistent issues go to OKX support via live chat or ticket.

### KYC tiers and what they unlock

## OKX Trading Fees: What You Actually Pay

### Standard fees for regular users

OKX uses a maker/taker fee model. For regular (non-VIP) users, the standard rates are:

| Market | Maker Fee | Taker Fee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spot | 0.080% | 0.100% |
| Futures (USDT-margined) | 0.020% | 0.050% |
| Options | Tiered | Tiered |

Maker fees apply when your limit order sits in the order book and gets filled by someone else. Taker fees apply when you place an order that matches immediately against existing orders (market orders, or limit orders that cross the spread). Makers pay less because they add liquidity; takers pay more because they remove it.
